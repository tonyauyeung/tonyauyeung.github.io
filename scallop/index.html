Abstract

Recent progress in flow-based generative modeling has led to models that output high-quality samples while using only a small number of function evaluations. However, at present, there is a lack of similar advances in estimating the model likelihood. In particular, most existing methods either rely on restrictive architectures that enable exact calculations, or use stochastic approximations such as Hutchinson’s trace estimator that introduce substantial variance.

In this work, we introduce SCAlable LikeLihood distillation of flOw maPs (SCALLOP). SCALLOP builds on the recently proposed F2D2 (Ai et al.), a likelihood flow map model that can generate samples and their densities in a small number of function evaluations. While F2D2 uses Hutchinson’s estimator during training, we introduce an alternative and more scalable likelihood distillation objective that is Hutchinson-free and admits a vectorized formulation.

Empirically, we demonstrate the effectiveness of SCALLOP as a Boltzmann generator in molecular science, and further validate its benefit on image datasets. SCALLOP significantly reduces both training variance and training time while consistently improving performance compared to F2D2, and is competitive with the state-of-the-art while achieving up to 10× inference speedup over the fastest baseline.

Method

Samples and likelihoods, learned together.

Samples + log density

xs, log ps(xs)

Pretrain the velocity

Learn a velocity field that transports Gaussian noise toward the data distribution. Use this pretrained model as the teacher for a likelihood flow map.

Conditional Divergence Matching:
Distill without Hutchinson

Replace stochastic divergence estimation with conditional divergence matching: a vectorized objective that reduces training variance and computation.

Generate and reweight

Update the sample and its estimated log density together in a few network evaluations. Use these likelihoods for self-normalized importance sampling.

The key change is the likelihood training objective. At inference, a shared network predicts the sample update and log-density change without computing a full Jacobian.

Vectorized conditional divergence matching

View formulation

For the linear interpolation xt = (1 − t)x0 + tx1, with Gaussian noise x0 ∼ 𝒩(0, I) and a data sample x1 ∼ pdata, SCALLOP trains a vector-valued predictor Dθ ∈ ℝd using:

ℒCDM-v(θ;t)= 𝔼x0,x1 [ ‖ Dθ(xt,t,t)+ x01−t⊙ (vt(xt)−(x1−x0))− 11−t𝟏d ‖ 2 ]. \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CDM\text{-}v}}(\theta;t)=\mathbb{E}_{x_0,x_1}\left[\left\|D_\theta(x_t,t,t)+\frac{x_0}{1-t}\odot\bigl(v_t(x_t)-(x_1-x_0)\bigr)-\frac{1}{1-t}\mathbf{1}_d\right\|^2\right].

Here, ⊙ is elementwise multiplication, 1d is the all-ones vector, and vt is the pretrained velocity teacher. This objective replaces Hutchinson’s stochastic trace estimator with a coordinatewise training signal. The vector output targets the negative diagonal of the velocity Jacobian; summing its entries gives the scalar log-density-change prediction.

Δlogp^=(s−t) ∑j=1d Dθ,j(xt,t,s). \widehat{\Delta\log p}=(s-t)\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{\theta,j}(x_t,t,s).

In practice, the CDM-v loss is weighted by (1 − t) and combined with flow matching and self-distillation. Training samples t from [0, 1 − ε], with ε = 10−5, to avoid the endpoint singularity.

Results

Faster likelihood evaluation. More stable training.

On alanine systems, SCALLOP improves on F2D2 across all reported metrics and achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art Boltzmann generators.

Training stability and efficiency

Sampling and density evaluation are up to 10× faster than the normalizing-flow baseline from SBG and 100× faster than FALCON.
